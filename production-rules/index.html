Production rules

This document specifies the RETE-based deductive rule engine (logic-set): the rule form, its instructions, the trigger model, and aggregation/grouping with :accum.

1. Overview and Core Philosophy¶

Air blends symbolic code-as-data structures with deductive rule evaluation. A rule is a single structured form whose conditions are an implicit conjunction and whose instructions say what to derive and what side effects to run. Consequences are written as explicit :> slots, so the engine can track each derived fact across firings; aggregation is a condition-region :accum declaration rather than boilerplate.

(rule (student class=?c grade=?g)
      :accum (g=:mean)
      :> (average class=?c value=?g))

Rules are asserted into a logic-set like any other value:

(assert s (rule (parent ?x ?y) :> (ancestor ?x ?y)))

2. The Rule Form¶

(rule cond1 cond2 … :if g :let (k=v …) :> f :+ f :- f := f …)

  • Conditions are the leading elements up to the first instruction keyword. They are an implicit conjunction: (rule (parent ?x ?z) (ancestor ?z ?y) …). (not p) requires that no matching fact exists. :accum may appear anywhere in the condition region and applies to the whole conjunction.

  • Instructions are keyword/argument pairs interpreted in order:

    • :if cond — if the reduced condition is not truthy, none of the instructions that follow are executed. A :> skipped this way counts as having produced naught.

    • :let (k1=v1 k2=v2 …) — evaluate and bind names as in let. Bindings within one map are parallel; separate :let instructions are sequential, so later ones see earlier values.

    • :> form — a consequence. Reduced with the bindings, it must yield a fact or naught. Each :> has a fixed slot index (its position among the :> instructions) that identifies the derived fact across firings.

    • :+ form, :- form, := form — side effects run on an assertion, retraction, or revision trigger, respectively. They may call assert, retract, revise, or revise-fact only with an explicit reason=; :> needs none, because its output is automatically a derived fact.

  • At least one condition and one instruction are required. Rules enter a logic-set held and are never reduced as code.

Triggers¶

The condition decides the trigger; the consequences do not:

eventwheninstructions that run
assertiona new combination of conditions matches:if, :let, :>, :+
revisiona matching combination persists but a matched fact was revised (or an accumulator group changed):if, :let, :>, :=
retractiona matching combination stops matching:if, :let, :- (old bindings)

In :=, the special symbol _ is bound to a map-form of the revised condition wildcards to their new values.

Consequence slots and fact events¶

Facts carry stable identity tokens. Each (invocation, :> slot) remembers its last output, and the old/new pair classifies the event:

  • naught → fact: assert the fact with derived provenance.

  • fact → naught: retract it when no other reason holds it.

  • two different facts: revise — if the old fact has no other reason, its identity moves to the new value; otherwise the old fact stays and the new one is asserted.

  • equal facts: no event.

Only :> outputs drive downstream events. A revised fact keeps its identity, so a rule matching it fires its revision trigger (:=) rather than being retracted and re-asserted. Consequences therefore decide their own fact events; the condition event only selects which side effects run.

3. Reasons¶

A fact exists while it has at least one reason record:

  • a base record, labelled with the reason= given to assert (default naught) or a keyword; and

  • a derived record, (because RULE PREMISE-FACT…), one per deriving (invocation, :> slot).

Labels may be shared across facts and an assertion is its own record. Retracting or revising one record leaves the fact present if another reason still holds it. (why s fact) returns every reason. revise and revise-fact target base facts only.

4. Accumulators and Grouping (:accum)¶

:accum lists the accumulated variables. Every other bound variable is a group key.

A. Vectors¶

(rule (student class=?c grade=?g)
      :accum (g)
      :> (all class=?c grades=?g))

?c is a scalar group key; ?g binds to a vector of the group’s grades.

B. Per-variable aggregation¶

(rule (student class=?c grade=?g) :accum (g=:mean) :> (average class=?c value=?g))

Unannotated listed variables stay vectors. The aggregators are :min, :max, :sum, :mean, :median, :first, and :last.

C. Row selection with :take¶

:take (min v), :take (max v), :take :first, and :take :last bind all listed variables to the selected row’s scalars instead of vectors:

(rule (student class=?c grade=?g) :accum (c g :take (max g))
      :> (best class=?c grade=?g))

:take :last is optimized to track the latest match directly rather than collecting every value.

D. Nesting¶

Repeated :accum specs nest. Outer levels partition; only the final level aggregates or takes, yielding parallel sequences. In

(rule (student class=?c dept=?d grade=?g)
      :accum (d) :accum (g=:mean)
      :> (roll class=?c depts=?d means=?g))

?c is the scalar group key, ?d binds to the sequence of departments for that class, and ?g binds to a sequence of mean grades aligned with ?d. An annotation or :take on a non-final level is an error.

E. Ordering¶

:first and :last follow assertion order. A revision preserves a fact’s position in its group.

5. Filtering and Thresholds (HAVING Equivalent)¶

:if gates the instructions, so an aggregated group can be filtered before its consequence fires:

(rule (student class=?c grade=?g)
      :accum (g=:mean)
      :if ((mean ?g) > 85)
      :> (honor-roll class=?c avg=(mean ?g)))

Flipping a group in and out of the guard asserts and retracts the consequence, respectively.

6. Revision¶

(revise s condition changes) rewrites every base fact matching condition; changes is a (k=v …) map from the condition’s wildcards to new values. (revise-fact s old new) names the facts directly. Both take an optional reason= naming which record moves. A condition matching nothing is a no-op; matching only derived facts is an error.

(revise s (stats player=?p health-points=?h) (h=90) reason=:damage)

A revision moves the fact’s reason record and identity, so the fact is not retracted and re-asserted: downstream rules receive a revision trigger and run their := side effects.

7. Atomic Blocks¶

(logic s edit…) applies a group of edits as one event. Reads inside the block see the set as it stood on entry, per-fact revision chains collapse within the call (a later revise follows the moved record), and an error rolls the whole block back. Two successive logic calls do not collapse.
